# REPORT-SUSPECT-NOTES: suspicion hunt in the footnote and bibliography family

This lane re-read the PHP reference for the footnote and bibliography family (parse and render of Footnote, Footnoteblock, Footnoteitem, Bibliography, Bibitem and Bibcite — the one render family no earlier suspicion lane had covered) as a skeptic reads inherited code, and tested each suspicion against fresh saved-page probes on `sandbox-for-codex`. Live Wikidot behavior is definitionally correct; the fossil code's shape is never evidence, only a hypothesis generator. Evidence order is ftml corpus and integration pins, frozen parity captures, then fresh live probes; a fresh valid source-matched probe outranks a frozen pin. Sources were fetched once, cached and reused. I did not edit ftml, did not set repo-local git identity variables, ran no cargo, and used only the tree's own `ref-php/harness/batch.php` for the reference side. The starting pins were the R4.1 adoption and R4-CORR records in GAP-LEDGER.md and conformance-refphp/notes/ (R4-C1 bibliography title escaping, R4-C2 the footnoteitem separator, R4-C4 the bibliography tail boundary) and the R2-NOTES batteries and cross-rule records; the bibcite per-page suffix and the Stage 2/framing whitespace differences are recorded surfaces of this family, not divergences.

Fourteen suspicions were raised and each was given two observably different predictions before any probe, in `conformance-suspect-notes/predictions.md`. Eight are CONFIRMED (the odd code is live's real behavior), two are UNREACHABLE from authored page source by a parse-side argument, and four are UNTESTABLE because their predictions are not observable on a saved page or the decisive source cannot be authored. No divergence was found, so no candidate `C-SUSPECT-NOTES-n` was raised, no reference file was changed, and no before-copy or CORRECTION note under `conformance-suspect-notes/` was needed. The CONFIRMED behaviors are written as nine Lean propositions in `lean-ftml/LeanFtml/SpecNotes.lean`, each with its probe page named above the statement; the file was built successfully in isolation.

Probe discipline. Ten pages were created under the permanently kept prefix `muse-probe-suspect-notes-` with `scripts/wikidot-probe` through `scripts/wikidot-python` against `sandbox-for-codex`, and captured with two independent anonymous GETs and a saved-source read-back. All ten captures in `conformance-suspect-notes/captures.json` are `valid=true`, `stable=true` and `saved_source_matches=true`. Three create attempts failed the tool's read-back verification and their pages are left in place but are not used as evidence: `muse-probe-suspect-notes-n-tab-break` (Wikidot rewrote the tab-only blank line), `muse-probe-suspect-notes-fnb-trail-nl` (Wikidot stripped the trailing LF) and `muse-probe-suspect-notes-bib-indent` (Wikidot stripped the leading space on the first line); the last was replaced by the successful second-line-indent page `muse-probe-suspect-notes-bib-indent2`. The reference side was produced in one batch with `php ref-php/harness/batch.php render` over the exact intended sources (`conformance-suspect-notes/probe-cases.jsonl` → `ref-render.jsonl`, empty stderr), with `bib-indent2` appended after its source was fixed. `conformance-suspect-notes/compare-report.txt` shows the byte comparison: before comparison I mask only the recorded bibcite per-page suffix, the recorded `&nbsp;` serialization of a non-breaking space, Stage 2 pretty-print whitespace including whitespace purely between tags, and the Stage 2 unwrap of `<p>` around a deflist block. All ten decisive surfaces agree; no rule-level byte difference survives.

## Suspicion ledger

### S1 — The one-off `$marked` flag in a three-paragraph note (CONFIRMED)

Construct: `Parse/Default/Footnoteblock.php:68-80` marks only the first `\n[ ]*\n` in a collected note body with a Footnoteitem `parabreak` token, on the stated theory that later paragraphs separate through the paragraph-end renderer. Why suspicious: a one-off boolean carrying state through a callback is exactly the sort of asymmetric guard that silently loses the third occurrence. Prediction A (code as suspected): `x[[footnote]]p1\n\np2\n\np3[[/footnote]]` joins p2 and p3 into one block. Prediction B (skeptic): all three paragraphs render separately. Reading the reference first already refuted my darker reading of A: its Stage 1 output is `p1\n<p>p2</p>\n\n<p>p3</p>`, so the code's real prediction is B. Probe: `muse-probe-suspect-notes-n3para`, with the two-paragraph control `muse-probe-suspect-notes-n2para-ctrl` and the earlier pin `muse-probe-r2notes-fnb24`. Live serves the superscript, then `p1`, `<p>p2</p>` and `<p>p3</p>` with separate newlines, byte-identical on the family surface to the reference (the difference is only the recorded Stage 2 inter-block newline framing). Verdict **CONFIRMED**: the single marker plus the paragraph renderer is live's real behavior, pinned in `suspect_notes_first_paragraph_break_only` and `suspect_notes_parabreak_renders_lf`.

### S2 — A tab-only blank line is invisible to the paragraph-break marker (UNTESTABLE)

Construct: the same `/\n[ ]*\n/` admits spaces between newlines but not tabs, while Prefilter's whitespace-only-line trim uses `\s`. Prediction A: `x[[footnote]]p1\n\t\np2[[/footnote]]` loses the break; Prediction B: it survives as for a space-only blank line. The reference renders the intended source as `p1\n<p>p2</p>` either way, so the two predictions may not even differ at the final surface, and independently the decisive source is not saveable: the create attempt `muse-probe-suspect-notes-n-tab-break` failed the read-back check twice because Wikidot rewrote the tab-only line. Verdict **UNTESTABLE**; reason recorded under U1 below.

### S3 — A blank residual line does not reset the Bibliography walk state (CONFIRMED)

Construct: `Parse/Default/Bibliography.php:72-73` returns `''` for a whitespace-only residual line but leaves `$prev` unchanged, so a blank line between two residual lines does not end a residual run. Why suspicious: state is carried between body lines with no reset on the empty-line case. Prediction A (code): `foo\n\nbar` inside a bibliography body joins as `foo bar`. Prediction B (skeptic): the blank line yields a line break or separate paragraphs. Probe: `muse-probe-suspect-notes-bib-res-blank` (`[[bibliography]]\n: a : A\nfoo\n\nbar\n[[/bibliography]]\n((bibcite a))`). Live serves `<div class="bibitem" id="bibitem-1">1. A</div>\nfoo bar</div>`, byte-for-byte the reference's decisive bytes apart from recorded framing and the bibcite suffix. Verdict **CONFIRMED**; pinned by `suspect_notes_blank_residual_keeps_prev`.

### S4 — The bibliography opener is line-anchored (CONFIRMED)

Construct: `Parse/Default/Bibliography.php:36` starts with `^\[\[bibliography` under `/m`; the earlier battery pinned `[[ bibliography]]` (space inside the brackets) and a trailing-tab closer but not indentation before the opener. Prediction A (code): an indented opener does not claim the block, so the block stays literal and `: a : A` becomes a definition list. Prediction B: the block still forms. The first-line attempt `muse-probe-suspect-notes-bib-indent` failed save verification because Wikidot stripped the leading space; the discriminating page `muse-probe-suspect-notes-bib-indent2` puts the same opener on the second line. Live serves `x<br />\n[[bibliography]]<br />\n<dl>\n<dt>a</dt>\n<dd>A</dd>\n</dl>` and an undefined-bibcite error span, exactly the reference's surface. Verdict **CONFIRMED**; pinned by `suspect_notes_indented_opener_unclaimed`.

### S5 — A lazy closer that does not end its line is not a closer (CONFIRMED)

Construct: the same regex requires `\[\[/bibliography\]\][ ]*$` with `/sm`, so a closer followed by prose cannot satisfy `$`, and the lazy `.*?` looks for a later closer. Why suspicious: `$` combined with `[ ]*` is a narrow tail that could have been an accidental fossil restriction. Prediction A (code): `[[bibliography]]\n: a : A\n[[/bibliography]] tail` with no later closer stays literal and a following bibcite errors. Prediction B: the block forms at the first closer. Probe: `muse-probe-suspect-notes-bib-close-tail`. Live keeps the literal opener and closer, forms the deflist and emits `<span class="error-inline">Bibliography item <em>a</em> not found.</span>`, matching the reference. Verdict **CONFIRMED**; pinned by `suspect_notes_closer_with_tail_unclaimed`.

### S6 — Whitespace asymmetry between definition values and residual lines (CONFIRMED)

Construct: `Parse/Default/Bibitem.php:47` only does `trim(str_replace("\n", " ", $content))` while `Bibliography.php:84` collapses `\s+` in residual lines; the registered value therefore can carry internal runs that the residual walk would collapse. Prediction A (code): `: a : A   B` keeps the three spaces in the value; Prediction B: it collapses to one. Probe: `muse-probe-suspect-notes-bib-multi-space` with control `muse-probe-suspect-notes-bib-ctrl`. Both live and reference serve `<div class="bibitem" id="bibitem-1">1. A B</div>`: the registered value keeps the bytes but the later Tighten fold shared by both collapses them before output. Verdict **CONFIRMED** (the collapsed surface is live's real behavior); the residual-line collapse law is pinned by `suspect_notes_residual_ws_collapse`, and the earlier pins are b44-leading-trailing-space and bib38-definition-separators.

### S7 — Reverse label-case mismatch (CONFIRMED)

Construct: `Bibcite.php:46` uses `/i` but preserves the authored label in `$matches[1]`; `Render/Xhtml/Bibcite.php:41` looks it up in a case-sensitive PHP array, and `Bibitem.php:45` also preserves case. The R2 battery pinned uppercase cite against lowercase definition (bibc-10), not the reverse. Prediction A (code): lowercase `((bibcite alpha))` against `: Alpha : Upper` errors. Prediction B: labels normalize and resolve. Probe: `muse-probe-suspect-notes-bibc-case-rev`. Live serves `<span class="error-inline">Bibliography item <em>alpha</em> not found.</span>` alongside `1. Upper`, byte-identical to the reference. Verdict **CONFIRMED**; pinned by `suspect_notes_bibc_case_sensitive_lookup` and `suspect_notes_bibc_error_echoes_label`.

### S8 — Footnoteitem's parse process is dead (UNREACHABLE)

Construct: `Parse/Default/Footnoteitem.php:43-49` declares `$regex = null` and a process() that reads an undefined `$options`; it is never called by the base scanner. Prediction A: `a[[footnoteitem]]b` stays literal; Prediction B: it claims a token or deletes the span. Parse-side argument: a null regex means the base `parse()` has nothing to scan, and the only Footnoteitem tokens are created directly by Footnoteblock's process through `addToken`, which does not call this process; the mirror records the pass as the identity (`footnoteitemRule_id`) and the earlier battery pin fnb-39-literal-footnoteitem settles the literal surface. Verdict **UNREACHABLE**; no new probe spent.

### S9 — Footnoteblock's duplicated empty-count guard (UNREACHABLE)

Construct: `Render/Xhtml/Footnoteblock.php:39-42` reads `vars['footnotes']` again and renders nothing on an empty collection, duplicating the parse-time guard at `Footnoteblock.php:53`. Prediction A: no authored source produces block tokens with an empty collection; Prediction B: a note collected after the block would leave an empty start token. Parse-side argument: Footnote runs at pipeline position 18 before Footnoteblock at 20, Include runs at position 1, and Footnoteblock's own process claims nothing when the collection is empty; the R2-NOTES cross-rule record X2 pins collection into the first block wherever it sits. Verdict **UNREACHABLE**; no probe spent.

### S10 — Dead locals in the Footnote renderer (UNTESTABLE)

Construct: `Render/Xhtml/Footnote.php:40-41` reads `$options['label']` (which Footnote::process never sets) and Math's process-static `$equationsArray` into an unused `$refs`. Why suspicious: cross-rule state carried into a renderer and ignored. Prediction A and Prediction B are not observably different on any saved page: neither value reaches the returned string, E_WARNING is suppressed by the harness and not served on a saved page, and the R2-NOTES harness audit records the Math static explicitly. Verdict **UNTESTABLE**; reason under U2.

### S11 — Bibcite's anchor id omits the id prefix (UNTESTABLE)

Construct: `Render/Xhtml/Bibcite.php:46` emits `id="bibcite-' . $id . '"` while the same token's scrollToReference uses `$idPrefix . $id`. Why suspicious: a nonempty prefix would make the anchor id disagree with its own target. Prediction A: no prefix in the id; Prediction B: prefix included. Not observable on live: the id prefix is render configuration, not page source, and is empty on every saved page in the sandbox (R2-NOTES Part 6 S1 records the only visible suffix class as the per-page random five-digit suffix, which is a recorded surface). Verdict **UNTESTABLE**; reason under U3.

### S12 — End-of-string `$` immediately before a final LF (UNTESTABLE)

Construct: `Footnoteblock.php:46` uses `|($)` with `/s` and no `/m`, so PCRE's `$` also matches immediately before a final LF; `NotesRules.lean` mirrors this by inserting the auto-appended footer at `src.length - 1` when the source ends in LF. Prediction A: the footer lands before the final LF; Prediction B: after it. The discriminating source cannot be authored: Wikidot strips a trailing LF on save, and the create attempt `muse-probe-suspect-notes-fnb-trail-nl` failed read-back verification. Verdict **UNTESTABLE**; reason under U4.

### S13 — `]]` inside a footnoteblock title (CONFIRMED)

Construct: `Footnoteblock.php:46` captures the attribute group as `(\s+[^\]]+)?`, which stops at the first `]`, so a title value containing `]]` truncates the opener and the tail becomes source. Prediction A (code): `[[footnoteblock title="A]]B"]]` uses the empty-title path (no title div) and leaves `B"]]` as literal text. Prediction B: the title is `A]]B`. Probe: `muse-probe-suspect-notes-fnb-title-brackets`. Live serves a footnotes footer with no `<div class="title">`, then `<br />\nB&quot;]]`, byte-identical to the reference. Verdict **CONFIRMED**; pinned by `suspect_notes_bracket_in_title_truncates`.

### S14 — Non-breaking space after the bibliography closer (CONFIRMED)

Construct: `Bibliography.php:36` ends with `[ ]*$`, admitting ASCII spaces only. Prediction A (code): a closer followed by U+00A0 does not match, so the whole block stays literal and a following bibcite errors. Prediction B: the block forms. Probe: `muse-probe-suspect-notes-bib-close-nbsp`. Live keeps the literal opener and closer, forms the deflist and emits the not-found span, exactly the reference's family bytes; live writes the NBSP as `&nbsp;` where the reference has the raw byte, which is the recorded serialization surface and not a divergence. Verdict **CONFIRMED**.

## Untestable and unreachable suspicions with reasons

- **U1 (from S2):** the `/\n[ ]*\n/` versus `\n\s*\n` choice has no observable consequence on `p1\n\t\np2` because the earlier whitespace-only-line trim normalizes the tab-only line before either form decides, and the decisive source is not saveable: `muse-probe-suspect-notes-n-tab-break` failed the read-back verification twice. Reason: predictions not observably different, plus serving-layer save normalization.
- **U2 (from S10):** the unused `$label` and `$refs` in the Footnote renderer never affect the returned bytes, and PHP's undefined-key warning is not a saved-page surface. Reason: predictions not observably different.
- **U3 (from S11):** the `id_prefix` asymmetry needs a non-empty render configuration, which cannot be reached from authored page source on the sandbox; the only visible bibcite id variation is the recorded per-page suffix. Reason: no live page-source observation can discriminate.
- **U4 (from S12):** PCRE's `$` before a final LF cannot be exercised because Wikidot strips the trailing LF on save; `muse-probe-suspect-notes-fnb-trail-nl` failed read-back verification. Reason: decisive source not authorable.
- **UNREACHABLE S8 and S9:** both are parse-side arguments given in full above — a null regex whose process is never called, and a render-time guard whose empty state cannot be produced by the pipeline order — not untestable ambiguities.
