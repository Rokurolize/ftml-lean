# REPORT-SUSPECT-EMAIL — suspicion hunt in the Email family

This lane re-read the Email parse and render rules as inherited code and tested every suspicion against fresh saved-page probes on `sandbox-for-codex`, with live behavior definitionally correct and the fossil's shape used only to generate hypotheses. Scope: `ref-php/text-wiki/Text/Wiki/Parse/Default/Email.php` (address regex, described pass, standalone pass) and `ref-php/text-wiki/Text/Wiki/Render/Xhtml/Email.php` (`str_replace('@','|', strrev($email).'#'.strrev($text))` inside the wiki-email span), mirrored by `emailLocalB`, `emailDomB`, `atomChainLen`, `emailMatchLen`, `lazyTail`, `emailDescrMatch`, `emailStandaloneMatch`, `emailRule` in `lean-ftml/LeanFtml/Inline1Rules.lean` and by `renderEmail` in `lean-ftml/LeanFtml/InlineSimpleRules.lean`. The method is the one in BRIEF-SUSPECT-NOTES and the completed SUSPECT lanes: write two observably different predictions before probing, use the smallest discriminating saved-page shape, compare byte by byte against the reference, and verdict CONFIRMED, DIVERGENCE (candidate `C-SUSPECT-EMAIL-n`, full record, optional in-tree correction with a before-copy under `conformance-suspect-email/`), UNREACHABLE, or UNTESTABLE. The pre-probe predictions are preserved verbatim in `conformance-suspect-email/DESIGN.md`; nothing there was edited after probing. Scope boundary: the address classes below are the Email rule's own regex grammar only — `[_a-z0-9\-]+(?:\.[_a-z0-9\-]+)*@[a-z0-9\-]+(?:\.[a-z0-9\-]+)+` with `/i` — and its described-label continuation. Mail transport is out of scope: the bundled PHPMailer 2.0.0 rc1 under `lib/phpmailer/` and its Ozone `PHPMailerWrap`/`OzoneEmail` wrappers are never called by the Text_Wiki Email rule, and the coordinator's canonical external-library inventory in GAP-LEDGER.md is settled ground for those libraries, Text_Highlighter's snapshot and libtidy. No probe here was chosen for transport-side address handling, PHPMailer behavior or generic RFC validation.

Coordinator steering was applied after the first draft: REPORT-ANTIGRAVITY-SUSPECT.md section 2.2 in the sibling tree `~/muse-bhl/lean-ftml-antigravity-suspect/` settles the family's core semantics with seven named probes (`p18-email-html`, `p19-email-utf8`, `f01-email-ee`, `f02-email-apos-amp`, `x03-email-url-label`, `x04-email-bracket-label`, `x05-email-raw-label`). I did not create any new probe for those shapes. Instead I verified them independently and cheaply: I read their seven valid/stable/source-matching captures under `conformance-agy-suspect/` byte for byte, and I re-ran all seven sources through this tree's `ref-php/harness/transform.php` on PHP 8.5.4; the reference bytes reproduce their reported reference outputs exactly, including `<i>` for `p18`, `A9 C3` for `p19`, raw `&` for `f02`, and raw `0xFF` digits for `x03`/`x05`. Their capture for `f01` actually contains U+FFFD + `é` + U+FFFD (their prose summary renders it as bare `é`), which agrees exactly with the bytewise-reversal-plus-substitution mechanism. Their settled verdicts are folded below with the Agy probe names cited alongside this lane's; where their record changes a disposition from my first draft (the U+FFFD substitution and the leaked-placeholder digits were called frame surfaces there), their DIVERGENCE verdict wins per the evidence order.

Probe discipline for this lane. Forty-four pages were created under the permanently kept prefix `muse-probe-suspect-email-` with the bundled `wikidot-probe` CLI (42 in the first batch, two index-shift pages in a second batch), read back against their intended source, and double-fetched anonymously; all 44 captures are `valid=true`, `stable=true` and `saved_source_matches=true`. The first single-run capture attempt hit a transient connect timeout after 26 pages and wrote no file, so all 42 were recaptured in six retried chunks and merged; the chunk logs and the merged file are retained. Reference renders were produced on this tree with PHP 8.5.4 through `ref-php/harness/transform.php` with raw byte I/O, plus `batch.php render`/`tokens`; `batch.php`'s `json_encode` silently drops the five records whose render contains invalid UTF-8, which is itself a direct observation of the bytewise-reversal construction, so the raw-byte driver is the one used for every comparison. Comparison is reported at three levels: exact raw bytes (29 of 42 first-batch probes match), decoded text with invalid bytes replaced (34 of 42), and decoded text with the remaining frame surfaces set aside; the first two levels expose the render divergences recorded as candidates below, while the third isolates the whitespace and index-numbering frame surfaces from them. No page was deleted, no ftml file was edited, no git identity was set, and no reference file was changed.

## What the decisive captures show

S-EMAIL-1 and S-EMAIL-11 are two halves of one settled render rule. The bytewise reversal is real: for `[u@example.com café]` this lane's reference serves `A9 C3 66 61 63` and Agy's `p19-email-utf8` (`[a@b.com café]`) serves the same shape; for `[a@b.com éé]` their `f01-email-ee` capture gives live `U+FFFD é U+FFFD` against the reference's raw `A9 C3 A9 C3`, which is exactly what UTF-8-with-substitution decoding of the reversed bytes produces. Where this lane first called the difference a frame-level substitution, the Agy record settles it as a render DIVERGENCE, and their `p18-email-html` and `f02-email-apos-amp` supply the independent reason: live applies `ENT_NOQUOTES`-style escaping after reversal (`&`, `<`, `>` escaped; `"` and `'` raw), while `Render/Xhtml/Email.php:29` performs no escaping at all. I confirmed the reference half of all four sources independently on this tree (`moc.b|a#<i>`, raw `&`, raw `A9 C3`), so the differing bytes are the reference's `<`/`>`/`&` and invalid bytes against live's `&lt;`/`&gt;`/`&amp;` and U+FFFD. My `lbl-at`, `lbl-hash` and `lbl-pipe` pins remain CONFIRMED: `@`→`|` runs over the whole concatenation and the `#` join is literal, because none of those characters is in the escaped set.

S-EMAIL-4 settles the open X44 question, and the Agy captures corroborate it: live does **not** bound the email label the way X44 bounded the interwiki label. Every exotic label shape claimed on both sides: `a]b]` closes at the first `]` and leaves `b]` literal; `a[b` keeps the opening bracket (this lane's `lbl-open-bracket`, Agy's `x04-email-bracket-label`, whose live and reference outputs are byte-identical); `http://example.org/x` as a label is consumed as an earlier Url placeholder (this lane's `lbl-url`, Agy's `x03-email-url-label`); a newline is consumed as the already-emitted Newline placeholder; `@@raw@@` is consumed as a Raw placeholder (Agy's `x05-email-raw-label`); another email, `**bold**`, a leading `]` and two described forms in one paragraph all behave exactly as the lazy PHP scan says. The parse-side over-claim is therefore CONFIRMED on both records. The render side diverges in exactly one way: the reference emits raw `0xFF` + digit + `0xFF`, live emits U+FFFD + digit + U+FFFD, and live's digit is consistently one higher (this lane: 2/3 for `lbl-url`, 0/1 for `lbl-newline`, 3/4 for `idx-url-shift`, 1/2 for `idx-newline-shift`; Agy: `\xff2\xff` → `3` in `x03`, `\xff0\xff` → `1` in `x05`). The shift probes prove this is a fixed token-numbering offset rather than a different claim: adding one earlier token raises both sides' digits by one while preserving the gap. Agy records the render difference as DIVERGENCE (candidate C-SUSPECT-EMAIL-2 here); the digit itself is a numbering frame surface, but the raw delimiter bytes are a genuine render defect.

S-EMAIL-5 and S-EMAIL-6 settle the separator and trailing-space questions together. A tab is expanded to spaces before Email runs, so `[u@example.com<TAB>Label]` forms a described claim on both sides with three spaces in the token option; a non-breaking space after the address forms no described claim on either side (both serve the obfuscated bare address followed by the literal bracketed remainder), so the separator really is one literal ASCII space. Three spaces and a trailing space in the label are preserved in the reference token option and served with the spaces reversed to the tail; live hoists that trailing whitespace out of the span and then applies the ordinary page-level whitespace collapse, as `frame-trail-bracket` shows directly (`(<span …>lebaL</span> )` against the reference's `(<span …>lebaL </span>)`) and `frame-trail-inline` shows before a following word. This is the same frame-level trailing-space trim that the frozen p07 capture recorded and that the parity side declined to pin bytewise; it is not an Email rule divergence, and the label token itself is identical on both sides (`gap-trail` is a raw-byte match). The one remaining text difference in this group is that live serializes a literal non-breaking space as `&nbsp;` where the reference writes the raw UTF-8 bytes `C2 A0`; that is a serving-layer entity choice on a shape Email does not claim, not Email behavior.

S-EMAIL-7 through S-EMAIL-10 are raw-byte matches across all fourteen address-boundary probes and all five `/i` and adjacency probes: `a@b.c` claims; `u@localhost`, `u@example_com.net`, `u@example..com`, `u@@example.com` and `u.@example.com` stay literal; leading and trailing domain hyphens, a numeric TLD and a trailing dot after the address behave as the character classes say; plus-addressing claims only from `tag@`; uppercase ASCII claims; the Kelvin sign and a non-ASCII byte adjacent to the local part do not enter the address but the following ASCII substring does; and an email inside a described label produces exactly one span. The Lean mirror's `emailMatchLen`, `emailLocalB` and `renderEmail` therefore describe live on every class probed here, with the render escaping caveat above.
