Suspicion families. Family A are fresh third-order siblings of the REFCORR11 general C0-control rule, raised only because its mechanism changed: REFCORR10 applied the C0 filter inside the single-authored-break branch, while REFCORR11 moved a single unconditional preg_replace to before the break predicate and before the empty-Raw visible projection in both Footnote and Bibitem. Moving the filter ahead of /[^\n]\n(?!\n)/ means a control byte that previously witnessed an authored LF as the sole non-LF byte is now removed before the predicate runs, which can flip the branch and change a rule-level <br />. Family B are re-probes of the three shapes that REFCORR11 was built to fix; each carries an explicit ledger reason tied to the changed mechanism (verify the general rule actually converges on post-REFCORR11 ground, which a correction cycle claims but has not been re-measured here). Family C are fresh heading/TOC/anchor shapes, including the C0 scope boundary where REFCORR11 did NOT change any mechanism, plus structural TOC placement and anchor-id siblings.
