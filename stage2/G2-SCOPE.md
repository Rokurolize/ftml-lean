# G2-SCOPE: the Stage 2 transformation inventory, settled from live evidence

Stage 2 is the transformation Wikidot applies between the Stage 1 render (the reference PHP in `ref-php/`, differentially validated at the parse level in R2/R3) and the served page: in live Wikidot this is the `libtidy`-based purification pass (`WikiTransformation::purifyHtml`). This inventory was settled by differential probing: every rule below is pinned by named probe shapes whose live captures validate (valid, stable, saved_source_matches all true) and whose reference Stage 1 output, run through the executable model in `stage2-model/`, reproduces the live capture byte-for-byte after stripping outer ASCII whitespace. Probe artifacts live in `stage2-evidence/` (batteries A, B, C) and in `conformance-refphp/stage2/` (the original 34 authored probes). Probe pages remain on sandbox-for-codex under the prefix `muse-probe-stage2-`.

## T1. Frame stripping, tag and attribute normalization

The Stage 1 document frame is stripped and tags are re-serialized canonically: tag names are lowercased, attribute lists are re-spaced to single spaces (`<div  class="a">` and `<div >` both normalize), and a literal NBSP character in text becomes the entity `&nbsp;`. Numeric character references emitted by Stage 1 (`&#8212;`, `&#8230;`, `&#160;`, `&#171;`) pass through unchanged. Pinned by p33 (NBSP inside a span becomes `&nbsp;`, numeric entities preserved) and p34 (model-side normalization of `<div  class="a" id="u-b" style="color:green">`). Attribute ORDER is not normalized by Stage 2: it preserves the order Stage 1 emitted, pinned for spans by q27 and q28 (`id`, `class`, `style` in emitted order survive verbatim). The bound on this rule is C-STAGE2-1: for `div`, live serves `id` before `class` where the reference Stage 1 emits `class` before `id`, so the divergence sits in Stage 1's attribute assembly for the div rule, not in Stage 2 (see REPORT-STAGE2.md).

## T2. Same-tag span-family crossings are settled in Stage 1, not Stage 2

For crossings inside the span family (color, size, strikethrough, underline spans, which all render as `<span>`), the Stage 1 output is already properly nested: the Stage 1 pairing rule (G1.2, process depths) closes the innermost open element with the same HTML tag name, so a `##` closer closes the most recent span regardless of which span construct opened it, and no Stage 2 re-nesting occurs. Pinned by p01 through p08 and p14 (span-family crossings in both orders, including color/size and size/strikethrough permutations), where the model's repair pass leaves the Stage 1 nesting untouched and matches live exactly. The bound: this holds only within the same-tag family; crossings between distinct tags are Stage 2 work (T3, T4).

## T3. Single-crossing rotation (one element above the target)

When a closer's target has exactly one open element above it on the stack, Stage 2 closes the crossed element, closes the target, and reopens the crossed element before the next non-whitespace content; leading whitespace of the following text is hoisted outside the reopened element. Pinned by p17 (`<strong>OUTER <em>INNER</strong> TAIL</em>` becomes `<strong>OUTER <em>INNER</em></strong> <em>TAIL</em>`), q02 (the same shape nested inside an outer color span), q03 (the crossed `em` contains a closed `tt` child; rotation still fires), q06 (strong/color: the crossed span reopens), q07 (color/em), q08 (em/color: the crossed span reopens), q25 (the same rotation inside a heading), and q26 (inside a blockquote paragraph). The whitespace hoisting is pinned by p27 (a space at a closing inline's edge emerges outside the closer as `</span> y`). Two bounds are established. First, the anchor bound: when the target is an `a` element, the crossed element closes outright and never reopens (q10: `<a href="/x">A <strong>B</a> C</strong>` becomes `<a href="/x">A <strong>B</strong></a> C`). Second, the reassignment bound: when the crossed element contains a closed span child and a span is also open below the target, the closers do not rotate at all; each closer closes the innermost open element whatever its tag (pure stack-pop reassignment), pinned by p16 (`<span color><strong><em><span u>text</span>1</strong>2</em>3</span>` becomes `...<em>text</span>1</em>2</strong>3</span>` with no reopening anywhere). The discriminating configuration between rotation and reassignment, as far as the evidence reaches, is the presence of that closed span child inside the crossed element together with the open span below; q03 bounds it on the other side (a closed `tt` child does not trigger reassignment).

## T4. Multi-element crossings (two or more elements above the target)

When a closer's target has two or more open elements above it, the crossed elements close innermost-first, the target itself stays open, and the crossed non-span elements reopen in pop order before the next content, which reverses their nesting; spans among the crossed elements are closed and never reopen. Pinned by p12 (at `</strong>` with `tt` and `em` crossed: both close, strong stays open, they reopen as `<tt><em>` around the following text, and the later `</em>` and `</tt>` closers close the reopened elements in turn), p13 (at `</sup>` with `strong` and `sub` crossed: reopen as `<strong><sub>`, nesting reversed), q04 (at the color span's `</span>` with `strong` and `em` crossed: both close, the span stays open and wraps the rest of the paragraph, they reopen as `<em><strong>`), and the first crossing of p15 (at `</strong>` with a size span, an `em`, and a color span crossed: all three close, only the `em` reopens). The bound is the p15 tail, which is unsettled: after that crossing, the later `##` closer (whose span target was already dropped) closes the whole remaining inline chain (`</em></strong>`), and the later `</em>` closer reopens an `em` around the following text; no rule in this inventory predicts that pair of behaviors, and the settling probe shapes are named in REPORT-STAGE2.md as remainder U1.

## T5. Inline propagation across block boundaries, and the table-cell scope wall

An inline element left open at a block boundary is closed at the boundary and reopened inside the next block, as long as its own closer has not yet appeared: pinned by p18, p19, and p20 (reopening across `li`, `dt`/`dd`, and `div` boundaries) and by q25/q26 for heading and blockquote containers. Table cells are a hard scope wall: an inline opened in one cell is closed at the cell boundary and does not reopen in the next cell, pinned by p21, p22, and p23. A color span whose markers straddle a paragraph boundary in the source is not an element at all in Stage 1 output (the markers render literally) and Stage 2 changes nothing there, pinned by q20.

## T6. Paragraph unwrap around block content

A `<p>` wrapper vanishes when its body contains a block-level trigger (img, table, dl, div, ul, ol, blockquote, pre, h1 to h6, hr) and the body's block structure is either fully balanced or contains at least one balanced block pair beside any unclosed starts; the paragraph's flow content then joins the surrounding flow directly. Pinned by p29 (paragraphs around an image with text, a table, and a definition list all unwrap) and by corpus case in2-x48 (a paragraph wrapping the head of a collapsible block unwraps even though three divs are still open at its `</p>`, because two balanced div pairs complete inside the body). The bound is p09: a paragraph whose only block content is a single unclosed `div` start with no completed pair keeps its wrapper, and T7 then applies instead. A paragraph with no block trigger keeps its wrapper (the ordinary case throughout the corpora, and p30's segmented shapes). The fitted boundary between p09 and in2-x48 is recorded as remainder U2 in REPORT-STAGE2.md with its settling shapes.

## T7. A block start closes an open paragraph

When a block-level element starts while a `<p>` is open (and the paragraph was not unwrapped under T6), the paragraph is closed before the block start; a displaced `</p>` later in the stream is dropped as stray. The same closing fires for `hr` and for `pre`. Pinned by p09's second half (Stage 1 emits `<p>A<br />\n<div align></p>`; live serves `<p>A<br /></p>\n<div align>`, the paragraph closed and kept, the stray `</p>` gone) and by q15 (an `hr` following paragraph text leaves the paragraph closed before it).

## T8. Segment joining and newline layout

After unwrapping and repair, block-level siblings are joined by exactly one newline, and block parents place a newline after their opening tag and before their closing tag when the adjacent child is block-level; this is the F-R2-58 surface A family. Pinned by p32 (the block-joining newline matrix), q11 through q15 (text paragraph followed by list, blockquote, heading, code block, and rule, each joined by a single newline with Stage 1's blank lines removed), q16 (nested divs with paragraphs), q31 (bibliography and footnotes-footer blocks laid out with single newlines), and the R4-C4 case (see REPORT-STAGE2.md). The flow-joining bound: two unwrapped flow segments join with no inserted space at all, pinned by p29 (`mid` and `text` from adjacent unwrapped paragraphs serve as `midtext`).

## T9. Space-run collapse and inline-edge hoisting

Runs of whitespace in flow text collapse to a single space, and a space sitting at the inner edge of an inline element is hoisted outside it; whitespace inside `pre`, `code`, and raw spans is preserved exactly. Pinned by p26 (Stage 1 already collapses runs inside a segment; Stage 2 handles the edges the same way), p27 (closing-edge hoisting), and p28 (pre/code/raw preservation). 

## T10. Empty-element pruning

An empty prunable inline (span family, strong, em, and their kin) is removed, and a paragraph left empty is removed with it; an empty `div` and an empty `a` are kept. Pinned by p31 (empty span and its paragraph vanish; empty div and empty anchor survive) and by p09's tail (an empty paragraph opened at the end of a div leaves no trace in live output).

## T11. A lone break between block siblings

A `<br />` that sits directly after a block-level sibling (no intervening text node) and directly before another block-level element is dropped. Pinned by corpus case tab-x16 (Stage 1 emits `</table><br />\n<table>` for two adjacent tables; live serves `</table>\n<table>`). The bound is corpus case tab-x36: a `<br />` that forms its own segment, separated from the preceding block by a text node, is kept (`</div>\n<br />\n<table>` serves unchanged). Wider bounds (other block pairs, breaks at a parent's edges) are remainder U3 in REPORT-STAGE2.md.

## T12. Stray closers and edge balancing

A closer whose element is not open is dropped (p09's displaced `</p>` under T7 is the pinned instance in flow content; over-closed shapes in the contain corpus agree throughout). Elements still open at the end of their containing block or at document end are closed innermost-first: pinned by p12 (the strong, held open by the multi-crossing rule, is closed at the paragraph's end) and by the boundary behavior in T5. Source-level stray markers are not Stage 2's concern: unclosed or over-closed wikitext constructs whose markers Stage 1 renders as literal text (q21 through q24) pass through unchanged.
